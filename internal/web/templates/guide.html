How Archivis scores photos

Every measurement is objective and repeatable: it is computed from pixels, not from opinions, and photos are resampled to a 1600 px long edge first so numbers are comparable across cameras. Scores are meant for triage (finding the sharp, well-exposed frames among thousands) not as a verdict on a picture — a deliberately soft, dark, or warm image will score low on the dimension it breaks.

Focus

Measured on 16×16 tiles after a light noise-suppressing filter: each tile's Laplacian (edge) energy is divided by the tile's own contrast, so a hazy, dark or low-contrast photo is not mistaken for a blurred one, and the estimated noise contribution is subtracted. The score uses the sharpest 10% of tiles, so a sharp subject against a blurred background still counts as in focus. When a face is a prominent subject, the sharpness of the aligned face crop dominates (70%): a crisp background behind a soft face is a missed shot. Calibrated on real photos with known applied blur: a Gaussian blur of 1 pixel scores about 0.4, 2 pixels or more scores 0.

Exposure

Penalises clipped highlights (more than 1% of pixels at 250+), crushed shadows (more than 4% at 5 or below), a mean luminance far from the middle, and a compressed tonal range (flat or hazy frames). Low-key and high-key images are penalised by design: filter with care.

White balance / colour cast

The colour of the light is estimated from image edges (the "grey-edge" method of van de Weijer et al.), which is less fooled by large coloured objects than averaging pixels. The cast is reported as the angle between that light colour and neutral grey, with its direction (warm/yellow, cool/blue, green, magenta). Half of ordinary photos measure under 3°, which counts as neutral; a 10% colour shift reads about 4–5°, and 12° or more is a strong cast. Black & white images are detected and exempt. A single photo cannot always tell a coloured light from a coloured scene (a sunset, a yellow wall), so treat it as a flag, not a verdict.

Noise

Immerkær's noise operator, averaged over 16×16 blocks, read from the flattest tenth of the frame and reported as the standard deviation in 8-bit levels. Using only flat regions keeps fine texture (foliage, fabric, hair) from being mistaken for noise.

Technical rating

A model trained on human judgements: 10,000 photos from the BIQ2021 database, each rated by 30 people. It combines the measurements above (and their interactions, so that exposure is penalised at both extremes) to predict how people rate a photo's quality, on a 0–100 scale. On 2,000 photos it never saw during training, its ranking agrees with the human ratings with a Spearman correlation of 0.63, and it separates the worst-rated quarter from the best-rated quarter 92% of the time. The individual focus, exposure, colour and noise scores remain available as filters.

Aesthetic

How appealing people are likely to find the photo, as their predicted average rating out of 10 (most photographs land between 4 and 7.5). It is a linear model on CLIP image embeddings, fit to the ratings of about 30 people per photo on 4,070 photographs (the EVA dataset). On photos it never saw it agrees with people about as well as one group of raters agrees with another. It is the one subjective component: treat it as a second opinion, not a verdict.

Teaching it your taste

On any photo page, say whether the aesthetic score is too low, about right or too high, or rate the photo yourself from 1 to 10. Each judgement is saved with the score you were shown and the models behind it. archivis aesthetic train fits a new model to your judgements, starting from the current one so that a few dozen ratings nudge it rather than replace it. It is tested on ratings it was not fit to, and only joins the blend if it predicts those better. Tested against real people's own ratings, 20 judgements cut the error for that person by about a fifth, mostly by learning how they use the scale. How photos rank against each other improves steadily from about 100 judgements. Run archivis aesthetic models to see the models.

Models are never discarded. Every photo keeps each model's score, and the aesthetic score shown is a weighted blend of the active models. Each model is named after a fingerprint of its weights, so a score always belongs to the exact model that produced it. A new model scores your photos from their stored embeddings in seconds, with no re-indexing, and earlier scores stay as they were.

Overall

Overall = half technical rating, half aesthetic (3/10 counts as 0, 8/10 as 100). Photos without a CLIP embedding use the technical rating alone.

Faces

Faces are found with SCRFD and compared with ArcFace (InsightFace buffalo_l). Similarity is cosine: about 0.5 or more is very likely the same person, 0.35–0.5 is possible, and different people are usually under 0.25. Confirmed labels are used as exemplars to label matching faces automatically (similarity ≥ 0.45 and clearly ahead of anyone else); automatic labels are marked “?” and never override a confirmed one.

Categories and text search

CLIP (ViT-B/32) embeds every photo. Text search compares your words to those embeddings; categories are zero-shot matches against short descriptions you can customise in categories.txt in the data directory (then run archivis retag).
